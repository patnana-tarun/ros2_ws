**Reading.**
- **Against the last confirmed state (the tag): much better.** 90 % comes 2.5–3× sooner
  (5–9 min against 17–21 min, and one tag run never got there). 95 % is now reached in every run;
  before, no run ever passed 91 %. The robot is stuck 2–8 % of the time instead of 10–33 %, and it
  follows its path more closely: heading p90 in the open is 10–16° against 13–20°, cross-track p90
  3–4 cm against 5–7 cm.
- **Against today's HEAD (yesterday's unconfirmed rotation-shim WIP): more reliable and more
  accurate, a little faster.** Most of the speed came from the shim, which removed most of the
  standing still. But HEAD stopped early in 2 of 8 runs and swung ±30–40° after every shim turn.
  Today's changes make the speed reliable: every run reaches 95 %, with no swings and better
  tracking. HEAD's good runs (95 % in 9.7–11.5 min) overlap with the candidate's 7.9–12.6 min.
- **Rotation events: none under the user's definition.** Under the old definition (net displacement)
  there was one per run, against 0 / 1 / 0 for the tag: a faster robot that explores a short pocket
  (turn in, back out, turn round) packs ≥ 300° into 60 s while ending near where it started, and the
  tag escaped this only by standing still 40–54 % of the time. Those trips are not spinning, so on
  2026-10-21 the user changed the definition to path driven < 1 m in the 60 s. Real spins on the spot
  still count. See "Rotation events that remain".
- Caveats: the tag runs used the older recorder (`run_demo.sh`, stop at 90 %, 40 min cap) on another
  day; the metric definitions are the same. Today's HEAD and candidate runs were interleaved.
  "Goal success" is not compared: the candidate drops goals whose frontier was explored on the way,
  which are not failures. Goals Nav2 gave up on: candidate 0–3 per run, HEAD 1–4.

## What changed, in plain words
1. **Steering no longer overshoots.** The path controller (MPPI) plans its next moves from how fast the
   robot is *measured* to turn. In the simulation the robot turns about 1.4× faster than commanded, and
   MPPI was only allowed to change its turn rate slowly (`az_max` 2.0). So after every turn it kept
   turning past the path, then corrected back: a ±30–40° wobble. Letting MPPI change the turn rate
   faster (`az_max` 6.0, both controllers) removed the wobble. The real command is still smoothed by
   the velocity smoother, so the motors see the same limits as before.
2. **No more false "exploration complete".** When every remaining frontier had failed once, the
   explorer declared the cave explored and stopped. Now it retries them once the robot has moved.
3. **Frontiers ahead are preferred.** A frontier behind the robot costs a penalty in the frontier
   choice, so the robot does not U-turn for a small gain.
4. **Drop goals that are already explored.** Frontiers are often the LiDAR shadow behind a boulder;
   they disappear as the robot drives towards them. The coordinator now drops such a goal and picks
   the next one from where the robot is, instead of driving into the pocket and turning round.
5. **Small fixes:** a frontier the LiDAR can never see into (inside the goal tolerance) is no longer
   "reached" again and again; backing out of a tunnel stops once the robot is in open space; goals
   are placed two cells inside known space.

What did not work: moving goals away from walls (the walls the robot hasn't seen yet are unknown
on the map, so the goal couldn't tell how close they were). It was removed.

## Ceiling: is 95 % reachable at all?
The planner plans for a round robot of radius 0.22 m ([[Tunnel-reverse 2026-10-20]]). Robot centres
it can reach = floor cells more than 0.22 m from a wall, boulder or stalagmite, connected to the spawn.
From each, a 7.5 m LiDAR (the SLAM range) was ray-cast over the true cave geometry: the floor field
`field.npz` plus the 13 boulders and 26 stalagmites as the cave generator places them (seed 7,
re-run to record the stalagmite positions; `assets/navigation/nav-speed/ceiling.py`).

| Planner radius | Reachable centres (share of floor) | Floor visible from them (ceiling) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.22 m | 58 % | **98.5 %** |
| 0.27 m (+5 cm map margin) | 49 % | 98.4 % |
| 0.11 m (inscribed, the old planner) | 82 % | 99.0 % |

Chamber F is not reachable for a 0.22 m planner, but it is visible from the end of the C–F tunnel.
What stays unseen is boulder interiors and a corner behind the boulder in chamber F (1.5 %).
**95 % is not ruled out by geometry.** A first estimate that took stalagmites from the occupied cells of
39 earlier final maps gave 92.5 %: map noise near walls looked like stalagmites. Don't use maps
for obstacle geometry.

## Plans "through unknown cells"
The metric checks each `/plan` against the global costmap rosview last received (published at 1 Hz).
A new diagnostic (`plan_unknown_detail` in `track.npz`) shows that every flagged point lies inside
the costmap, near the goal end of the path, and that rosview's costmap copy was 1.1–1.9 s old. Checked
against the **next** costmap, every flagged plan has **0** unknown points: the planner (`allow_unknown:
false`) planned through cells the map had just revealed, and rosview's copy had not caught up.
This is a timing artefact of the measurement, not a path through unknown space. The metric
definition is unchanged; the gate still counts these.

Not all of it is timing. In the second confirmation, cand_2 has 78 flagged plans from one goal at the
narrow chamber-F entrance (13.79, −5.09). For 30 s the goal was replanned at 2 Hz and every plan
ended on a cell that stayed unknown in the following costmaps as well. NavFn with
`allow_unknown: false` should not end a plan on an unknown cell, so the planner's costmap and the
published one seem to differ there. Not explained; it needs the planner's own cell values at plan
time.

## Rotation events that remain
Every remaining event is ≥ 300° within 60 s with < 1 m net over a short **out-and-back trip**:
2.8–5 m driven into a pocket or dead end and back. It is made of a shim U-turn (90–270°),
reverse-controller turning while backing out (110–180°), sometimes Nav2's Spin recovery (≈ 90° twice
at a failing goal in a tunnel), and the coordinator's retrace. Thresholds are hit narrowly (300, 300,
301, 305°). The baseline had fewer such events per minute only because it stood still more
(the tag runs: 40–54 % of the time), so its turns were spread over more than 60 s.

In the second confirmation each candidate run had exactly one event: a Spin recovery in the SW
dead-end tunnel plus backing out (302°), a U-turn pair in chamber B (377°), and the start-up
(300°: turn round for the west dead end, back out, turn round). The heading-aware choice does not
stop the west-first start: with only 3 frontiers at the spawn the west one still scores best.

## Verdict
The candidate (e547cf2) is faster and tracks better than HEAD: 95 % in 7.9–12.6 min, every
run ≥ 90 % within 9 min, and no false "complete". The user accepted the result for [[Comparisons]]
(entry 14) and changed the rotation-event definition to path driven < 1 m in 60 s, under which all
runs pass. Plans through unknown cells are now counted only if the cell is still unknown in the next costmap
(the user's decision): that passes in 2 of 3 runs. cand_2 still fails with 74 plans to one goal at the
chamber-F entrance. No tag until that is fixed and confirmed.
